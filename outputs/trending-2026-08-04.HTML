Editor's Take

The week's standout signal is that agents are no longer the product — the infrastructure around them is. YC's qm hit 10k stars in under a week by treating multi-agent coordination as a multiplayer problem, not a prompt-chaining one. Meanwhile, two repos from the same author (ratchet and humanizer-cli) address the output-quality crisis from opposite ends: one verifies that agents follow their own rules, the other flags AI-written text before it ships. The 30-day window confirms that on-device inference keeps shrinking: Gemma 4 26B now fits in 2 GB on a MacBook, and Kimi K3's 2.78T parameters run on a single CPU in 8 GB via pure C99. If you are building agent tooling, the market has split — you are either making agents more capable or making their outputs more trustworthy. Both sides are hiring.
