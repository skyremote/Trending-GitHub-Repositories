Editor's Take

The week's signal is unmistakable: the AI ecosystem has pivoted from building agents to equipping them. Seven of the top ten repos are agent skills or agent infrastructure — not new models, not new frameworks, but specialized tools that make existing agents measurably better. Autoprompt claims 45% fewer task failures; JetBrains' benjamin-plus shows 17.9% cost savings with quality unchanged. The era of "trust me, it works" skills is giving way to quantified results. Meanwhile, the agent workspace is getting a proper desktop layer: Cumora treats agents as Slack-grade team members, OpenBot gives each one its own computer, and Wake lets you browse every session you've ever run. Hardware didn't go quiet — apex-inference-chip is real RTL running a real LLM on FPGA, not another benchmark slide. The 30-day view confirms it: when the top repo is a mascot-logo skill with nearly 4,000 stars and a book-to-skill converter clears 1,200, the platform war has moved from "can agents work" to "what should they do next."
